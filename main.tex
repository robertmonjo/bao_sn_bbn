\begin{abstract}
The hyperconical universe, embedded in a five-dimensional Minkowski space, provides an exact coasting expansion $a(t)\propto t$ on each constant-time slice. However, a distorted stereographic projection with a single shape parameter $\alpha$ fits to the DESI DR1 compressed BAO data ($z=0.295$--$1.491$; $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=0.68$ relative to flat $\Lambda$CDM). By setting both the high- and low-redshift limits of the running index $\alpha(z)$ as free parameters, the hyperconical model yields a primordial helium fraction ($Y_p=0.2454(34)$, within $0.03\sigma$ of the observed $0.2453\pm0.0034$) and a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio ($\mathrm{D/H}=2.527(30)\times10^{-5}$) at the nucleosynthesis window $T=0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$, with $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}= -2.9$ relative to the flat $\Lambda$CDM reference.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The persistent tension between early- and late-time measurements of the Hubble constant \cite{verde2019tensions}, reinforced by baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument first data release (DESI DR1; \cite{adame2025desi}), motivates geometrically grounded alternatives to $\Lambda$CDM in which apparent acceleration arises from a projection map rather than from a cosmological constant. In hyperconical approaches, an apparently accelerated phenomenology arises from the projection map, even when the underlying ambient-space expansion remains asymptotically linear \cite{monjo2018projected, monjo2023hubble}.

Coasting cosmologies, for which the late-time scale factor ($a$) behaves as $a(t)\propto t$, have been studied extensively by John et al. \cite{john2000coasting, John2019}, and more recently they have reappeared in hyperconical modified-gravity constructions where the linear law is tied to an ambient-space geometric constraint \cite{monjo2017hyperconical, Monjo_2024, Monjo_2025}. Recent hyperconical constructions show good agreement with cluster and lensing data \cite{monjo2025clusters, monjo2025lensing}.

The main cosmological motivation for departing from strict coasting at high redshift comes from big bang nucleosynthesis (BBN). Two primordial abundance tracers constrain the expansion rate at the BBN epoch: the helium-4 mass fraction $Y_p=0.2453\pm0.0034$ \cite{Aver2021} and the deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio $\mathrm{D/H}=(2.527\pm0.030)\times10^{-5}$ \cite{schoneberg2024bbn}. At neutron freeze-out ($T_{\rm freeze}\approx0.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$), the $n/p$ ratio is set by $H(T)/H_{\rm std}$ and translates directly into $Y_p$; $\mathrm{D/H}$ then depends on synthesis rates at $T\approx0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and on the baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta$ \cite{kolbturner1990,fields2020bbn,schoneberg2024bbn}. Under exactly linear expansion, $H(t)\propto1/t$, the freeze-out temperature shifts toward lower values, reducing the $n/p$ ratio and yielding $Y_p$ substantially below the observed $Y_p$ (see \cite{kolbturner1990} for the freeze-out mechanism; the quantitative result is derived in Section~\ref{sec:results}).

Like de Sitter and anti-de Sitter spacetimes, the hyperconical universe is a null quadratic locus in pseudo-Euclidean ambient space \cite{hawking1973large, monjo2017hyperconical}, yielding linear expansion on each constant-$t$ slice and providing a geometric interpretation for the coasting law. A distorted stereographic projection of this null cone, parametrised by $\alpha$, maps the cone's geometry to an effective four-dimensional distance-redshift relation in which inferred comoving distances exceed the coasting expectation; an observer using standard distance estimators would therefore infer an apparently accelerating expansion without a cosmological constant \cite{monjo2018projected, monjo2023hubble}.

In the following, we describe the hyperconical model and its distorted stereographic projection (Section~\ref{sec:methods}), constrain $\alpha$ against DESI DR1 BAO data, and test a running-$\alpha$ extension against the BBN window (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Discussion follows in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Data and methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Datasets}

The primary observational input is the DESI DR1 compressed BAO catalogue \cite{adame2025desi}, which provides measurements of three distance combinations in six tracer bins at effective redshifts $z_{\rm eff}=0.295$, $0.510$, $0.706$, $0.930$, $1.317$, and $1.491$. Each bin reports: (i) the comoving angular diameter distance ratio $D_M/r_s$, where $D_M(z)=(1+z)D_A(z)$ is the comoving transverse distance; (ii) the Hubble distance ratio $D_H/r_s \equiv c/[H(z)\,r_s]$; and (iii) the volume-averaged distance ratio $D_V/r_s \equiv [z\,D_M^2(z)\,D_H(z)]^{1/3}/r_s$. Here $r_s$ is the comoving sound horizon at the drag epoch. The two Ly$\alpha$ forest measurements at $z_{\rm eff}\simeq2.33$ ($D_M/r_s$ and $D_H/r_s$) are excluded from the fit: the Ly$\alpha$ forest probes intergalactic hydrogen absorption rather than galaxy clustering and carries additional systematic uncertainties (continuum estimation, metal-line contamination) not present in the galaxy and QSO tracers; combining them would require a dedicated cross-covariance treatment beyond the scope of this work. The fit therefore uses $N_{\rm BAO}=10$ data points; the excluded Ly$\alpha$ measurements are displayed as open symbols in Fig.~\ref{fig:bao_fit} for reference.

A complementary constraint is provided by the Pantheon$+$ compilation \cite{scolnic2022pantheon}, which contains 1701 light curves of 1550 distinct Type~Ia supernovae (SN) spanning $0.001<z<2.26$. We use the $N_{\rm SN}=50$ binned pairs of compressed redshift and comoving-distance proxy from \cite{Brout_2022}: each bin provides a dimensionless quantity $d_{\rm proxy}(z_i)\propto D_C(z_i)$ derived from the standardized distance moduli, with a single overall amplitude $A$ marginalized analytically at each $\alpha_{\rm high}$ (see Section~\ref{sec:methods:likelihood}). 

Finally, for the BBN comparison, two primordial-abundance measurements are used ($N_{\rm BBN}=2$): the helium fraction $Y_p=0.2453\pm0.0034$ \cite{Aver2021} and the deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio $\mathrm{D/H}=2.527\pm0.030\times10^{-5}$ \cite{schoneberg2024bbn}.

\subsection{Model}

\subsubsection{The hyperconical universe}

The hyperconical universe is the null locus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hyperconical}
\mathcal{H}^4:=\bigl\{(t,\vec\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{1,4}:\,-t^2+\|\vec\rho\|^2=0\bigr\},
\end{equation}
giving a scale factor $a(t)=t$ and coasting expansion $E(z)=1+z$ on each constant-$t$ slice. The intrinsic geometric coordinate $r$ on the cone is related to the observed redshift through the metric-determined map $\log(1+z)\mapsto r$ \cite{monjo2017hyperconical}, independently of the projection parameter $\alpha$.

\subsubsection{Projected prescription}\label{sec:proj}

A distorted stereographic remapping \cite{monjo2018projected} applied to the hyperconical model gives the projected comoving distance
\begin{equation}
\hat r :=
2\arctan\!\left[
\frac{\gamma(r)/2}{\left(1-\gamma(r)/\gamma_U\right)^\alpha}
\right],
\end{equation}
where $\gamma(r):=\arcsin(r)$, $\gamma_U=\pi/3$ for the hyperconical domain with spatial curvature $k=1$, and $\alpha$ is the distorted-projection parameter. The projected Hubble rate is \cite{monjo2017hyperconical}:
\begin{equation}
\hat H(z):=\left(\frac{d\hat r}{dz}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\mathrm{proj}}(z)=\frac{\hat H(z)}{\hat H(0)}.
\end{equation}
In the high-$z$ limit,
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\mathrm{proj}}(z)\propto (1+z)^{1+2\alpha}\quad(z\gg1),
\end{equation}
so $\alpha=1/2$ would give the radiation-like law $H_{\mathrm{perc}}(T)\propto T^2$, a value motivated by the symplectic Lagrangian formulation \cite{monjo2023hubble, monjo2020lagrangian}.

A square-root interpolation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:running_sqrt}
\alpha(z)=\alpha_{\mathrm{high}}-\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{high}}-\alpha_{\mathrm{low}}}{\sqrt{1+z}},
\end{equation}
recovers $\alpha(0)=\alpha_{\mathrm{low}}$ exactly and $\alpha\to\alpha_{\mathrm{high}}$ for $z\to\infty$, with $\alpha_{\rm high}$ as a free parameter. Here $\alpha_{\rm low}\simeq0.283$ is fixed by projected hyperconical analyses at lower effective redshifts \cite{monjo2017hyperconical, monjo2018projected, Monjo_2024}, but it can be also fitted by the joint optimisation (Section~\ref{sec:results}). 

Specifically, four main experiments were considered here: two by constraining (i) $\alpha$ or (ii) $\alpha_{\rm high}$ as a single geometric parameter in chi-square minimisation using BAO+SN data, and two others by minimising it with respect to (iii) $\alpha_{\rm high}$ or (iv) the joint parameter set $\{\alpha_{\rm low}, \alpha_{\rm high}, \eta\}$ using BAO+SN+BBN data.

\subsubsection{Reference model}\label{sec:lcdm}

Flat $\Lambda$CDM serves as the reference throughout.  The Hubble rate is therefore $E_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(z)=[\Omega_r(1+z)^4+\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_\Lambda]^{1/2}$ with $\Omega_\Lambda=1-\Omega_m-\Omega_r$ in all comparisons.  In all fits, the radiation density is fixed to $\Omega_r h^2=4.18\times10^{-5}$ (corresponding to $T_{\rm CMB}=2.72548\,\mathrm{K}$; \cite{fixsen2009,fields2020bbn}). Here $h\equiv H_0/(100\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}})$, where $h=0.68$ \cite{Monjo_2024} enters the evaluation of $\Omega_r=\Omega_r h^2/h^2$ with negligible influence (below $0.1\%$) on $E(z)$, while the BAO nuisance $\beta=c/(H_0 r_s)$ is marginalized analytically in all fits (see Section~\ref{sec:methods:likelihood}). In the BAO+SN comparison, the single free parameter is $\Omega_m$. In the BAO+SN+BBN comparison, a second free parameter is the baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta$, fitted to the primordial $\mathrm{D/H}$ constraint \cite{schoneberg2024bbn}; the helium fraction $Y_p$ is then a prediction. 

\subsection{Statistics and likelihood analysis}\label{sec:methods:likelihood}

For BAO, the fit quality is measured by $\chi_{\rm BAO}^2=(\mathbf{D}^{\rm obs}-\mathbf{D}^{\rm model})^T C^{-1}(\mathbf{D}^{\rm obs}-\mathbf{D}^{\rm model})$, where $\mathbf{D}$ is the vector of measured distance ratios $\{D_M/r_s, D_H/r_s, D_V/r_s\}$ and $C$ is the DESI DR1 full covariance matrix across all BAO distance combinations. Nuisance parameters are eliminated analytically: for BAO the nuisance $\beta=c/(H_0 r_s)$ is minimized at each $\alpha$. For the SN data, $\chi^2_{\rm SN}=\sum_i [d_{\rm proxy}(z_i)-A\,D_C^{\rm model}(z_i)]^2/\sigma_{d,i}^2$, where $A$ is marginalized analytically. 

For the BAO+SN+BBN comparison, single-measurement residuals are appended: $\chi^2_{Y_p}=[(Y_p^{\rm model}-Y_p^{\rm obs})/\sigma_{Y_p}]^2$ and $\chi^2_{\rm D/H}=[(\mathrm{D/H}^{\rm model}-\mathrm{D/H}^{\rm obs})/\sigma_{\rm D/H}]^2$. In the BAO+SN-BBN experiment, $\chi^2_{\rm joint}=\chi^2_{\rm BAO}+\chi^2_{\rm SN}+\chi^2_{Y_p}+\chi^2_{\rm D/H}$ is minimised simultaneously using Nelder--Mead optimisation; for BAO+SN-only fits, the figure of merit is $\chi^2_{\rm joint}=\chi^2_{\rm BAO}+\chi^2_{\rm SN}$. 

The reduced chi-squared per observable is $\chi^2_{N,i} =\chi_i^2/N_i$, where $N_i$ is the number of observable-$i$ data points, and per experiment is $\chi^2_\nu=\chi_{\rm joint}^2/\nu$ with $\nu=N_{\rm tot}-n$, where $N_{\rm tot}$ is the number of data points and $n$ the number of free parameters in the experiment; $\nu_{\rm tot}=(N_{\rm BAO}+N_{\rm SN}-2)-n$ for BAO+SN fits and $\nu_{\rm tot}=(N_{\rm BAO}+N_{\rm SN}+N_{\rm BBN}-2)-n$ for BAO+SN+BBN fits. All quoted uncertainties are $1\sigma$; values in parenthetical notation refer to error in the last quoted digit. For model comparison, flat $\Lambda$CDM with free $\Omega_m$ and the same nuisance marginalizations is fitted to the same data, with the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC; \cite{burnham2002model}) adopting $|\Delta\mathrm{AIC}|<2$ as the threshold for indistinguishable model performance.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Constant-$\alpha$ baseline} Applying the projected model to the DESI DR1 compressed BAO data gives $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.375_{-0.016}^{+0.017}$ with $\beta=28.85$, $\chi^2_N=1.23$ ($N_{\rm BAO}=10$). Flat $\Lambda$CDM gives $\Omega_m=0.290$, $\chi^2_N=1.17$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=0.68$: the two models are statistically indistinguishable. The BAO-derived $\alpha_{\rm BAO}=0.375$ is higher than the values $\alpha\approx0.283$--$0.323$ found in projected hyperconical analyses at lower effective redshifts \cite{monjo2017hyperconical, monjo2018projected, Monjo_2024}, consistent with a running $\alpha$ that increases from late times toward the BBN epoch.

\paragraph{One-parameter model comparison} The constant-$\alpha$ model (best-fit $\alpha=0.320$ for BAO+SN) gives $\chi^2_{N,\rm BAO}=2.43$, substantially worse than the $\Lambda$CDM reference ($\chi^2_{N,\rm BAO}=1.61$; Table~\ref{tab:joint}), motivating the running-$\alpha$ extension. Among running-$\alpha$ configurations, the BAO+SN-only fit gives $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.425(26)$, $\langle H_{\rm perc}/H_{\rm rad}\rangle_{\rm geom}\approx1.09$, and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-1.86$. Enforcing the BBN normalisation $\langle H_{\rm perc}/H_{\rm rad}\rangle_{\rm geom}=1.000$ over $T=0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$ ($\sigma_H\approx4\%$, derived from $\sigma_{Y_p}/Y_p$ via $N_{\rm eff}$ propagation \cite{Aver2021,kolbturner1990}) pins $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.423(1)$ with $\chi^2_{N,\rm BAO}=1.27$ and $\chi^2_{N,\rm SN}=0.80$. A freeze-out calculation \cite{kolbturner1990} yields $Y_p=0.153(6)$, however, indicating that the geometric-mean proxy covers the late synthesis window but not the $n/p$ freeze-out at $T\sim0.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$. 

\paragraph{Three-parameter extension} Freeing $\alpha_{\rm low}$ and $\eta$ in addition to $\alpha_{\rm high}$ ($n=3$,  Table~\ref{tab:joint}) resolves this, yielding $\alpha_{\rm low}=0.255(16)$ and $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.4379(5)$, with $Y_p=0.2454(34)$ (matching the observed $0.2453\pm0.0034$ \cite{Aver2021} to within $0.03\sigma$) and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-2.9$ against the 2-par $\Lambda$CDM reference ($n=3$ against $n=2$), mildly favouring the hyperconical model. Fitting the primordial deuterium abundance \cite{schoneberg2024bbn} requires $\eta=7.725(78)\times10^{-10}$, ${\approx}\,27\%$ above the CMB-derived $\eta_{\rm CMB}=6.11\times10^{-10}$; the consistency of this $\eta$ within the hyperconical model remains a subject for future work. The $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=-2.9$ result is robust to the adopted $H_0$: re-optimising $(\alpha_{\rm low},\alpha_{\rm high},\eta)$ over $H_0\in[65,74]\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ gives $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}\in[-3.0,-2.8]$, with $\alpha_{\rm high}$ absorbing the shift in BBN expansion rate.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Geometric motivation} Across the late-time BAO and primordial nucleosynthesis epochs, the projected hyperconical model provides a geometrically motivated laboratory for coasting cosmologies, with one or two free parameters governing the running projection index $\alpha(z)$. Within the simplest class, a constant-$\alpha$ fit to the DESI DR1 BAO data gives $\alpha_{\mathrm{BAO}}=0.375$ ($\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=0.68$ vs flat $\Lambda$CDM), but the constant-$\alpha$ model performs substantially worse in a joint BAO+SN fit (Table~\ref{tab:joint}), motivating the running-$\alpha$ extension. The $\Lambda$CDM BAO--SN tension ($\Omega_m^{\rm BAO}=0.289(22)$ vs $\Omega_m^{\rm SN}=0.371(20)$, a $2.7\sigma$ internal discrepancy) further motivates this class of geometrically grounded alternatives. By contrast, the running-$\alpha$ model yields a single coherent BAO+SN solution: the SN data favour $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.30^{+0.07}_{-0.06}$ (SN-only profile minimum), whereas the BAO data alone give $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.45\pm0.03$; the two estimates differ by $\approx2\sigma$ and the joint optimum $0.42(3)$ is consistent with both, so no strong internal tension arises.

\paragraph{BBN synthesis} The running-$\alpha$ model (Eq.~\ref{eq:running_sqrt}) with $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.423$ matches the geometric-mean Hubble-rate normalisation over the synthesis window $T=0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$ ($\langle H_{\rm perc}/H_{\rm rad}\rangle_{\rm geom}=1.000$, $\sigma_H\approx4\%$ derived from $\sigma_{Y_p}$ \cite{Aver2021,kolbturner1990}). However, the model's expansion rate at $T\sim0.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$, corresponding to the neutron freeze-out epoch, is $H/H_{\rm std}\approx0.41$--$0.47$, significantly below the standard value. A Kolb--Turner freeze-out estimate \cite{kolbturner1990} gives $Y_p\approx0.16$, far below the observed $0.247\pm0.004$ \cite{Aver2021}; the geometric-mean proxy covers only the late synthesis window and does not constrain n/p freeze-out.

Freeing $\alpha_{\rm low}$ as a third parameter (Table~\ref{tab:joint}) resolves this: the joint fit yields $Y_p=0.2454(34)$ ($0.03\sigma$ from the observed value) and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-2.9$ vs the 2-par $\Lambda$CDM reference.

This requires baryon-to-photon ratio to be $\eta=7.725(78)\times10^{-10}$, about $27\%$ above the CMB-derived $\eta_{\rm CMB}=6.11\times10^{-10}$ \cite{fields2020bbn,schoneberg2024bbn}; whether this value is self-consistent within the hyperconical model at the CMB epoch is a subject for future work. Our results show that hyperconical geometry can reproduce an expansion history effectively equivalent to radiation domination during BBN, but they do not establish a geometric origin for the radiation bath itself. The BBN yields rely on the analytical Kolb--Turner freeze-out approximation \cite{kolbturner1990}; cross-validation with a dedicated network such as \textsc{PRyMordial} \cite{burns2024prymoridal} or \textsc{PArthENoPE} \cite{consiglio2018parthenope} is planned for future work. An extension in which both $\alpha$ and the projection angle $\gamma_U$ run with redshift is the subject of ongoing work.
