\begin{abstract}
The hyperconical universe, embedded in a five-dimensional Minkowski space, provides an exact coasting expansion $a(t)\propto t$ on each constant-time slice. A distorted stereographic projection with a single shape parameter $\alpha$ fits the DESI DR1 compressed BAO data ($z=0.295$--$1.491$; $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=0.68$ relative to flat $\Lambda$CDM). A running-$\alpha$ extension recovers the standard nucleosynthesis Hubble normalisation (geometric-mean ratio 1.000) at $T=0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$, with $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-3.85$ relative to the two-parameter $\Lambda$CDM reference, establishing coasting cosmologies as a viable framework for joint BAO--BBN analyses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The persistent tension between early- and late-time measurements of the Hubble constant \cite{verde2019tensions}, reinforced by baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data \cite{adame2025desi}, motivates geometrically grounded alternatives to $\Lambda$CDM in which apparent acceleration arises from a projection map rather than from a cosmological constant. In hyperconical approaches, an apparently accelerated phenomenology arises from the projection map, even when the underlying ambient-space expansion remains asymptotically linear \cite{monjo2018projected, monjo2023hubble}.

Coasting cosmologies, for which the late-time scale factor ($a$) behaves as $a(t)\propto t$, were first studied by John et al. \cite{john2000coasting, John2019}, and more recently they have reappeared in hyperconical modified-gravity constructions where the linear law is tied to an ambient-space geometric constraint \cite{monjo2017hyperconical, Monjo_2024, Monjo_2025}. Recent hyperconical constructions show good agreement with cluster and lensing data \cite{monjo2025clusters, monjo2025lensing}.

The main cosmological motivation for relaxing exact coasting at all epochs comes from primordial nucleosynthesis. Under strictly linear expansion, with $H(t)=1/t$, neutron freeze-out occurs under non-standard conditions, leading to helium overproduction unless additional assumptions are introduced \cite{sethi1999coasting,dev2002coasting}. Here we constrain the projected hyperconical model against the DESI DR1 BAO data and test a running-$\alpha$ extension against the primordial nucleosynthesis window.

Like de Sitter and anti-de Sitter spacetimes, the hyperconical universe $\mathcal{H}^4:=\{(t,\vec\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{4,1}: -t^2+\|\vec\rho\|^2=0\}$ is a null quadratic locus in pseudo-Euclidean ambient space \cite{hawking1973large, monjo2017hyperconical}, yielding linear expansion on each constant-$t$ slice and providing a geometric interpretation for the coasting law.

In the following, we describe the hyperconical model and the distorted stereographic projection (Section~\ref{sec:methods}), constrain $\alpha$ against DESI DR1 BAO data, and test a running-$\alpha$ extension against the big bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) window (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Discussion follows in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Data and methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Datasets}

The primary observational input is the DESI DR1 compressed BAO catalogue \cite{adame2025desi}, which provides measurements of three distance combinations in six tracer bins at effective redshifts $z_{\rm eff}=0.295$, $0.510$, $0.706$, $0.930$, $1.317$, and $1.491$. Each bin reports: (i) the comoving angular diameter distance ratio $D_M/r_s$, where $D_M(z)=(1+z)D_A(z)$ is the comoving transverse distance; (ii) the Hubble distance ratio $D_H/r_s \equiv c/[H(z)\,r_s]$; and (iii) the volume-averaged distance ratio $D_V/r_s \equiv [z\,D_M^2(z)\,D_H(z)]^{1/3}/r_s$. Here $r_s$ is the comoving sound horizon at the drag epoch; within the projected hyperconical likelihood it enters only as the combined nuisance $\beta=c/(H_0 r_s)$, analytically marginalized at each $\alpha$. The two Ly$\alpha$ forest measurements at $z_{\rm eff}\simeq2.33$ ($D_M/r_s$ and $D_H/r_s$) are excluded from the fit: the Ly$\alpha$ forest probes intergalactic hydrogen absorption rather than galaxy clustering and carries additional systematic uncertainties (continuum estimation, metal-line contamination) not present in the galaxy and QSO tracers; combining them would require a dedicated cross-covariance treatment beyond the scope of this work. The fit therefore uses 10 data points; the excluded Ly$\alpha$ measurements are displayed as open symbols in Fig.~\ref{fig:bao_fit} for reference.

A complementary constraint is provided by the Pantheon$+$ compilation \cite{scolnic2022pantheon}, which contains 1701 light curves of 1550 distinct Type~Ia supernovae spanning $0.001<z<2.26$. We use the 50-bin compressed comoving-distance proxy from \cite{Brout_2022}: each bin provides a dimensionless quantity $d_{\rm proxy}(z_i)\propto D_C(z_i)$ derived from the standardized distance moduli, with a single overall amplitude $A$ marginalized analytically at each $\alpha_{\rm high}$ (see Section~\ref{sec:methods:likelihood}).

\subsection{Model}

\subsubsection{The hyperconical universe}

The hyperconical universe is the null locus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hyperconical}
\mathcal{H}^4:=\bigl\{(t,\vec\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{1,4}:\,-t^2+\|\vec\rho\|^2=0\bigr\},
\end{equation}
giving a scale factor $a(t)=t$ and coasting expansion $E(z)=1+z$ on each constant-$t$ slice. The intrinsic geometric coordinate $x$ on the cone is related to the observed redshift through the metric-determined map $\log(1+z)\mapsto x$ \cite{monjo2017hyperconical}, independently of the projection parameter $\alpha$.

\subsubsection{Projected prescription}\label{sec:proj}

A distorted stereographic remapping \cite{monjo2018projected} applied to the hyperconical model gives the projected comoving distance
\begin{equation}
\hat r :=
2\arctan\!\left[
\frac{\gamma(r)/2}{\left(1-\gamma(r)/\gamma_U\right)^\alpha}
\right],
\end{equation}
where $\gamma(r):=\arcsin(r)$, $\gamma_U=\pi/3$ for the $k=1$ hyperconical domain, and $\alpha$ is the single shape parameter. The projected Hubble rate is \cite{monjo2017hyperconical}:
\begin{equation}
\hat H(z):=\left(\frac{d\hat r}{dz}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\mathrm{proj}}(z)=\frac{\hat H(z)}{\hat H(0)}.
\end{equation}
In the high-$z$ limit,
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{\mathrm{proj}}(z)\propto (1+z)^{1+2\alpha}\quad(z\gg1),
\end{equation}
so $\alpha=1/2$ would give the radiation-like law $H_{\mathrm{perc}}(T)\propto T^2$; the empirical value $\alpha_{\rm high}\approx0.422$ gives a slightly sub-quadratic asymptote but recovers the BBN normalisation through the integrated rate over the nucleosynthesis window \cite{monjo2023hubble, monjo2020lagrangian}.

A square-root interpolation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:running_sqrt}
\alpha(z)=\alpha_{\mathrm{high}}-\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{high}}-\alpha_{\mathrm{low}}}{\sqrt{1+z}},
\end{equation}
recovers $\alpha(0)=\alpha_{\mathrm{low}}$ exactly and $\alpha\to\alpha_{\mathrm{high}}$ for $z\to\infty$, with $\alpha_{\rm high}$ as the single free parameter. Here $\alpha_{\rm low}\simeq0.283$ is fixed by projected hyperconical analyses at lower effective redshifts \cite{monjo2017hyperconical, monjo2018projected, Monjo_2024}, and $\alpha_{\rm high}$ is determined by the joint BAO+SN+BBN optimisation (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Likelihood analysis}\label{sec:methods:likelihood}

Statistical analyses keep $\alpha$ as the single geometric parameter. The fit quality is measured by $\chi_{\rm BAO}^2=(\mathbf{D}^{\rm obs}-\mathbf{D}^{\rm model})^T C^{-1}(\mathbf{D}^{\rm obs}-\mathbf{D}^{\rm model})$, where $\mathbf{D}$ is the vector of measured distance ratios $\{D_M/r_s, D_H/r_s, D_V/r_s\}$ and $C$ is the DESI DR1 full covariance matrix across all BAO distance combinations. Nuisance parameters are eliminated analytically: for BAO the nuisance $\beta=c/(H_0 r_s)$ is minimized at each $\alpha$. For the supernova data, $\chi^2_{\rm SN}=\sum_i [d_{\rm proxy}(z_i)-A\,D_C^{\rm model}(z_i)]^2/\sigma_{d,i}^2$, where $A$ is marginalized analytically. The joint figure of merit is $\chi^2_{\rm joint}=\chi^2_{\rm BAO}+\chi^2_{\rm SN}$, with $\nu_{\rm BAO}=9$ and $\nu_{\rm SN}=49$ degrees of freedom. For model comparison, flat $\Lambda$CDM with free $\Omega_m$ and the same nuisance marginalizations is fitted to the same data, with the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC; \cite{burnham2002model}) adopting $|\Delta\mathrm{AIC}|<2$ as the threshold for indistinguishable model performance.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

Applying the projected model to the DESI DR1 compressed BAO data gives $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.375_{-0.016}^{+0.017}$ ($1\sigma$) with $\beta=28.85$, $\chi^2=12.33$, for 10 data points and 1 free parameter ($\beta$ analytically marginalized). Flat $\Lambda$CDM gives $\Omega_m=0.290$, $\chi^2=11.66$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=0.68$: the two models are statistically indistinguishable.

The BAO-derived $\alpha_{\rm BAO}=0.375$ is higher than the values $\alpha\approx0.283$--$0.323$ found in projected hyperconical analyses at lower effective redshifts \cite{monjo2017hyperconical, monjo2018projected, Monjo_2024}, consistent with a running projection index that increases from late times toward the BBN epoch.

Direct $\chi^2$ minimisation of the running-$\alpha$ model (Eq.~\ref{eq:running_sqrt}) over the joint BAO+SN data gives $\alpha_{\rm high}\approx0.425$, at which the BBN normalisation $\langle H_{\rm perc}/H_{\rm rad}\rangle_{\rm geom}\approx1.11$ is already within 11\% of the standard value \cite{schoneberg2024bbn}. Enforcing $\langle H_{\rm perc}/H_{\rm rad}\rangle_{\rm geom}=1.000$ over $T=0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$ constrains $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.422$, with $\chi^2_{\nu,\rm BAO}=1.418$, $\chi^2_{\nu,\rm SN}=0.816$, and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-3.85$ relative to the two-parameter $\Lambda$CDM reference (see Table~\ref{tab:joint}).

Table~\ref{tab:joint} contrasts four configurations: the constant-$\alpha$ baseline and three SAT-2 rows. The constant-$\alpha$ model (best-fit $\alpha=0.320$ for BAO+SN) gives $\chi^2_{\nu,\rm BAO}=2.70$, substantially worse than the $\Lambda$CDM reference ($\chi^2_{\nu,\rm BAO}=1.789$), which motivates the running-$\alpha$ extension. Among SAT-2 rows, the 0-parameter configuration ($\alpha_{\rm high}=1/2$ and $\sqrt{}$-exponent both fixed) has $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=+4.23$; the 1-parameter optimisation over BAO+SN gives $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.425$ and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-1.86$; and the BBN-constrained row ($\langle H_{\rm perc}/H_{\rm rad}\rangle_{\rm geom}=1.000$ over $T=0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$, $\sigma_H=4\%$ from $Y_p$ \cite{Aver2021,fields2020bbn}) gives $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.422$ and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-3.85$ against the 2-par $\Lambda$CDM reference. A simplified freeze-out calculation \cite{kolbturner1990} yields $Y_p\approx0.16$ for the model, indicating that the geometric-mean normalisation constraint captures the rate of the late synthesis window but does not reproduce helium-4 production without an additional mechanism to regulate the neutron freeze-out at $T\sim0.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$.
A two-parameter extension that frees $\alpha_{\rm low}$ in addition to $\alpha_{\rm high}$ (see Table~\ref{tab:joint}, last row of the BAO+SN+BBN block) resolves this: optimising to $Y_p=0.2453$ \cite{Aver2021} yields $(\alpha_{\rm low},\alpha_{\rm high})=(0.250,\,0.4364)$, with $Y_p=0.2452$ ($-0.02\sigma$) and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-2.40$ against the 2-par $\Lambda$CDM reference ($k=3$ vs $k=2$, $\Delta k=1$). Fitting the primordial deuterium abundance \cite{schoneberg2024bbn} requires $\eta=7.61\times10^{-10}$, roughly $25\%$ above the CMB-derived $\eta_{\rm CMB}=6.11\times10^{-10}$; the consistency of this $\eta$ within the hyperconical framework remains a subject for future work.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}\label{sec:discussion}

The projected hyperconical model provides a geometrically motivated framework for coasting cosmologies, with a single free parameter $\alpha_{\rm high}$ governing the running projection index $\alpha(z)$ across the late-time BAO and primordial nucleosynthesis epochs. Within this class, a constant-$\alpha$ fit to the DESI DR1 BAO data gives $\alpha_{\mathrm{BAO}}=0.375$ ($\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=0.68$ vs flat $\Lambda$CDM), but the constant-$\alpha$ model performs substantially worse in a joint BAO+SN fit ($\chi^2_{\nu,\rm BAO}=2.70$ vs 1.789 for $\Lambda$CDM), motivating the running-$\alpha$ extension. The SAT-2 parametrisation with $\alpha_{\rm high}=0.422$ achieves $\chi^2_{\nu,\rm BAO}=1.418$ and $\chi^2_{\nu,\rm SN}=0.816$, and matches the geometric-mean Hubble-rate normalisation over the synthesis window $T=0.07$--$0.10\,\mathrm{MeV}$ ($\langle H_{\rm perc}/H_{\rm rad}\rangle_{\rm geom}=1.000$, $\sigma_H=4\%$ \cite{Aver2021}), giving $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-3.85$ against the 2-par $\Lambda$CDM reference. However, the model's expansion rate at $T\sim0.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$---the neutron freeze-out epoch---is $H/H_{\rm std}\approx0.41$--$0.47$, significantly below the standard value. A Kolb--Turner freeze-out estimate \cite{kolbturner1990} gives $Y_p\approx0.16$, far below the observed $0.247\pm0.004$ \cite{Aver2021}; the geometric-mean proxy captures only the late synthesis window and does not constrain n/p freeze-out. Freeing $\alpha_{\rm low}$ resolves this: the two-parameter extension with $(\alpha_{\rm low},\alpha_{\rm high})=(0.250,\,0.4364)$ gives $Y_p=0.2452$ ($-0.02\sigma$) with $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm tot}=-2.40$ vs the 2-par $\Lambda$CDM reference. This requires $\eta=7.61\times10^{-10}$, about $25\%$ above the CMB-derived $\eta_{\rm CMB}=6.11\times10^{-10}$ \cite{fields2020bbn,schoneberg2024bbn}; whether this value is self-consistent within the hyperconical framework at the CMB epoch is a subject for future work. The internal $\Lambda$CDM BAO--SN tension ($\Omega_m^{\rm BAO}=0.289$ vs $\Omega_m^{\rm SN}=0.371$) further motivates this class of geometrically grounded alternatives. An extension in which both $\alpha$ and the projection angle $\gamma_U$ run with redshift is the subject of ongoing work.
